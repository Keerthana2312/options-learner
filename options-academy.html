1 · What is an option?

An option is a contract that gives you the right — but not the obligation — to buy or sell a stock at a fixed price (the strike price) before a deadline (the expiration date).

Think of it like a reservation with a deposit: you pay a small fee (the premium) to lock in a price. If the deal turns out great, you use it. If not, you walk away and only lose the fee.

The two types

CALL the right to BUY at the strike price → you want the stock to go UP ⬆️

PUT the right to SELL at the strike price → you want the stock to go DOWN ⬇️

🧠 Memory trick: you CALL a stock up to you (buy), you PUT a stock down away from you (sell).

5 · Buyers vs. Sellers

Every option has two sides. The buyer pays the premium and gets the right. The seller (or "writer") collects the premium and takes on the obligation.

⚠️ Selling a "naked" call has unlimited risk — the stock can rise forever. This is why beginners should start by buying options or selling covered ones. Compare both sides visually in the Payoff Lab.

6 · Why options lose value over time (Theta)

An option's premium = intrinsic value (real value right now) + time value (the "hope" that things improve before expiry).

Time value melts away every day — faster as expiration approaches. This decay is called theta. Drag the slider to watch an at-the-money option's value melt:

🧊 An option is a melting ice cube. Notice the curve gets steeper near zero — the last 2 weeks melt fastest. Buyers fight the clock; sellers profit from it.

7 · Volatility — the price of uncertainty

Volatility is how wildly a stock swings. More swing = more chance of a big payoff = more expensive options (both calls and puts).

Implied volatility (IV) is the market's guess of future swings, baked into the premium. When fear or excitement rises (e.g., before earnings), IV rises and options get pricier — even if the stock hasn't moved.

📌 Key trap for beginners: buying a call right before earnings, the stock goes up… and you still lose money. Why? IV collapses after the announcement ("IV crush") and that drop can outweigh the stock move. Play with the Vol slider in the Greeks Explorer to feel this.

Δ Delta — speed

How much the option price moves per $1 stock move. Δ 0.60 → option gains ~$0.60 when stock gains $1. Also a rough probability of finishing ITM.

Γ Gamma — acceleration

How fast delta itself changes. Highest at-the-money near expiry — that's where option prices behave most explosively.

Θ Theta — the melting clock

Value lost per day from time decay. Always works against buyers, for sellers. Accelerates near expiration.

V Vega — the fear gauge

Price change per 1% change in implied volatility. Long options gain when markets get nervous, lose when calm returns (IV crush).
